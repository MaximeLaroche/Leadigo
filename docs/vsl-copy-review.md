# VSL Page Copy Review — Leadigo (docs/index.html)

Audit against the VSL Copy standard: every claim paired with evidence, truthful next-step framing, no borrowed proof, no manufactured certainty.

## 1. Critical — contradictions and borrowed proof

### 1.1 "Every payment you make means you've already made money"
- **Where:** Hero shield callout (slide 1) and FAQ "How much does this cost?" (slide 14).
- **Problem:** Factually contradicts your own model. You charge per *booked* call — at that moment the client has NOT made money yet; they've bought a meeting, not a deal. A sharp buyer will catch this and it undermines the otherwise strong "pay per show" positioning.
- **Suggested rewrite:** *"No risk. You only pay when a qualified buyer books a call with you — every invoice means a real sales conversation is already on your calendar."*
- FAQ version: *"…it's structured so you only ever pay after a qualified buyer is booked on your calendar."*

### 1.2 Case studies are not your proof (slide 7)
- **Problem:** Phil Kirkeiner (Baton) and Sunny Stuart (Fithive) are third-party results from YouTube videos, presented under "The Proof / Why This Works At Scale." Framed this way, visitors will read them as Leadigo client results. That's borrowed proof and a credibility/legal risk.
- **Fix options:**
  1. Relabel the section as industry evidence: kicker → *"Industry Evidence"*, subhead → *"What companies achieve with professionally run cold email — independent examples, with sources."* Keep the YouTube links as citations.
  2. Add your own results block above it: `[NEEDS EVIDENCE: Leadigo client results — booked calls, time window, client type]`. Even one real engagement ("X calls booked for a [industry] client in N weeks") outweighs ten borrowed case studies.

### 1.3 "Your competitors are booking 20, 30, 50 calls per month" (slide 4)
- **Problem:** Specific quantitative claim with no source. Either attach evidence or generalize.
- **Suggested rewrite:** *"…they're booking calls with your ideal buyers every week using outbound systems that cost them nothing unless they work."*

## 3. Pricing transparency (FAQ, slide 14)

- **Current:** "The exact price depends on your industry and deal size…" — partially answers, then repeats the broken "already made money" line.
- **Standard:** disclose a range or the basis of the quote. A dodge here manufactures calls but also manufactures unqualified bookings — the opposite of your incentive-aligned pitch.
- **Suggested rewrite:** *"You only pay per qualified call that shows. Pricing is a flat per-show fee set on the strategy call based on your industry and deal size — most clients land between `[NEEDS EVIDENCE: typical per-show range]`. If a booked call doesn't fit your ICP, you don't pay for it."*

## 4. What's already strong — keep as-is

- **CTA honesty:** "Book a 15-minute call with me. Not a salesperson. Me. Zero obligation." — accurately describes the next step and matches the Calendly 15-min event. Message continuity is intact.
- **"It Doesn't Replace. It Multiplies."** (slide 6) — good objection handling; removes the "I already have channels" resistance without disparaging them.
- **FAQ "What if the leads aren't qualified?"** — the don't-pay-for-misfit answer is concrete and verifiable. This is your strongest trust asset; consider moving it into the Guarantee card on slide 13.
- **Timeline honesty:** "Setup takes 2-3 weeks… consistent flow by month 2" — realistic expectations, no instant-results hype.
- **No fake urgency or countdown tactics anywhere.** Keep it that way.

## 5. Structural notes

1. **Proof arrives before mechanism.** Slides 7 (proof) → 8 (logic) → 9 (math) is a good order for a skeptical, high-ticket buyer. No change.
2. **Slide 10's "cost of inaction"** leans entirely on hypothetical math. It lands much harder directly *after* one piece of real Leadigo proof — another reason to prioritize item 1.2.
3. **Founder section** is authentic but unproven as a credential: "2 years in software at an investment bank" isn't evidence of cold-email results. Either connect it explicitly (*"where I watched a sales org run on exactly this kind of system"*) or let the guarantee carry the trust.
4. **Missing asset:** a written summary / FAQ for buyers who won't watch the video. The page already forces a scroll-through read, so this is mostly covered — but consider captions or a "what the video covers in 60 seconds" blurb under the embed.
